Keep every statement. Rebuild your journals from them.

hledger-flow is a command-line workflow for hledger, the plain-text accounting tool. hledger keeps your accounts in journals: human-readable text files you can open in any editor, in a format that hledger reads and that Ledger and other plain-text accounting tools largely share.

hledger-flow is opinionated about where files live. You save each statement, exactly as your bank issued it, in a folder for its owner, institution, account and year. hledger-flow import converts each one with your hledger rules, or a script for formats rules can’t read, and links the journals through all-years.journal. The statements stay as permanent input, so every journal can be rebuilt from its source and every entry traced to the statement it came from. How does it compare with plain hledger?

A place for the account your other tools leave out

If your finance app doesn’t support a particular broker or wallet, that account disappears from your reports. In plain text, you can always record it. Read a trade history with hledger’s CSV rules, write a script for an unusual export, or enter a loan to a friend by hand in a manual journal. All three appear on the same balance sheet. To see investments at market value, add the prices you want hledger to use.

hledger-flow makes that work repeatable. Keep the original statements, exactly as your institutions issued them, and the rules that read them. Years later, in a tax audit say, you can show where any transaction came from. When you improve a category, run hledger-flow import again and every statement that uses the rule is converted again, in every year, with no search-and-replace through old entries. If you keep the generated journals in Git, you can review which postings changed before you commit.

Change a rule, rebuild every year

City Rail purchases have been landing in expenses:misc. Add a rule to import/mybank.rules and run hledger-flow import from the ledger root. Every City Rail purchase, in every year you’ve imported, moves to transport.

Handwritten transactions live in manual journals, so a rebuild never touches them.

The combined view belongs to you

hledger and hledger-flow run on your computer. You can produce a household balance sheet without connecting your bank to your broker or uploading either statement to an aggregation service. Public blockchain transactions remain public. The link between a wallet, your other accounts and your notes exists only in your own files.

Where statements and journals live

The folders tell hledger-flow who owns an account, where it’s held and which year you filed it under. The statement and its generated journal share a filename stem, so you can find the source of an entry. You add the statements, rules and manual journals. hledger-flow writes the rest, so you can delete every generated file and get the same ledger back.

Ink marks the files you write. Pencil marks generated files.

hledger-flow or plain hledger?

There are many ways to keep your finances with hledger, and hledger’s workflows page describes them. Three of them handle bank statements in quite different ways.

hledger import

  • Your journal is the primary record.
  • You classify, split and annotate entries by editing the journal.
  • A better rule changes future imports only.
  • Overlapping downloads are fine. hledger skips entries it has already imported, as long as their dates and same-day order stay stable.

How hledger import works

Read the CSV directly

  • The CSV files are the record. Nothing is generated.
  • You classify by editing the rules.
  • A better rule applies the next time hledger reads the file.
  • Suits a few files you name yourself, as in hledger -f checking.csv bal. The hledger 1.99 previews can also include a CSV in a journal.

Reading CSV with hledger

hledger-flow

  • The original statements are the primary record. Journals are rebuilt from them.
  • You classify by editing rules files.
  • A better rule rebuilds every journal that uses it, in every year.
  • A fixed folder for each owner, institution, account and year keeps years of statements maintainable. An account’s statements mustn’t overlap, or a purchase appears twice.

Quick start

Choose hledger import if your journal is your primary record and you want to edit it by hand. Choose hledger-flow if you want to generate your journals from statements exactly as they came from the source, classify by editing rules, and keep years of files in a structure that stays maintainable and auditable.

hledger-flow grew out of scripts written while using Dmitry Astapov’s Full-fledged hledger, whose wiki explains the ideas behind this approach in depth.
