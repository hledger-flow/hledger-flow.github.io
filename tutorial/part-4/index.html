Part 4: When rules aren't enough

This is the fourth part of a five-part tutorial. It continues in my-finances from part 3.

A savings account

Section titled “A savings account”

In October 2016 Gawie opens a savings account at Bogart Bank. Its statements are quarterly, and the first one runs from 28 October 2016 to 27 January 2017. It’s filed under 2017, the year the statement ends:

It has the same format as the cheque account, so the two accounts should share their rules. Gawie moves everything except account1 and the categories from bogart-cheque.rules into a new file, import/gawie/bogart/bogart-statements.rules. Each account’s rules file is now three lines.

import/gawie/bogart/cheque/bogart-cheque.rules:

import/gawie/bogart/savings/bogart-savings.rules:

And three more categories for import/gawie/categories.rules. They come after if Rent, so Rente (interest) gets the last word:

2016-01-02? That deposit was made on 2 January 2017. The date table gave every month the year 2016, so January jumped back a whole year, ahead of the deposits it depends on. The balance assertion noticed.

What rules can’t do

Section titled “What rules can’t do”

hledger reads a CSV one row at a time. A rule can use anything in the row it’s converting, but nothing from the rows around it. The year is in the statement period near the top, '28 Oktober 2016','27 Januarie 2017', and not in any transaction row. No rule can reach it.

This is what a preprocess script is for. If an account directory has an executable called preprocess, hledger-flow runs it on each statement and gives hledger the script’s output instead. The script gets the statement’s path and the path to write to, plus the bank, account and owner names.

The script should do only what rules can’t, and leave everything else to the rules. Gawie’s script adds the year to each date and changes nothing else. He saves it as import/gawie/bogart/add-year:

Both accounts need it, so each gets a preprocess link to the one script:

The dates now carry their year, '01 Mrt 2016', so the date table in bogart-statements.rules takes it from a second group instead of writing 2016. This is the whole shared file:

The preprocessed statements are in 2-preprocessed/, next to 1-in/ and 3-journal/. When the rules misbehave, they show exactly what hledger read.

January is back in 2017, and every balance assertion holds. A savings account that starts empty needs no opening balance.

Transfers waiting for their other half

Section titled “Transfers waiting for their other half”

The transfer balance report lists transfer accounts that don’t add up to zero. assets:transfers:creditcard isn’t there: both sides of every card payment are imported. The savings deposits came from the cheque account, but its statements for those months aren’t in the ledger yet. When Gawie imports them, he’ll classify their side of each deposit as assets:transfers:savings too, and this row will drop to zero. Until then, the report reminds him.

The story ends with part 5, and a file that isn’t a statement at all.
